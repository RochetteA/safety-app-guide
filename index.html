How to change things in the safety app

Plain step by step instructions for adding buttons, labels, pages, phone numbers, emails and links. No coding, and nothing goes through the App Store.

Start here

Five minutes of reading that makes every other section obvious.

Getting in

  1. On a computer, not a phone, go to macewan.apparmor.com. The editing screen puts a live phone preview beside your work, and that needs a wide screen.
  2. Click Sign in with MacEwan Credentials, or use the username and password your app administrator gave you. Forgotten it? Use Forgot Password? on the same screen.
  3. Open the Content Management tab, then click Designer. That is where every instruction on this site takes place.

Properties

Middle

App preview

Two habits that save you

Save constantly

Click Save Properties after every single thing you change. It does not save on its own. Click away from a button without saving and whatever you typed is gone.

Build in draft

Use Draft Mode while you work, and publish when it is finished. Published changes reach people's phones within seconds, so there is no quiet moment to leave something half built. Someone could open the app thirty seconds after you save.

How the app is put together

The app is a tree of buttons. The home screen is a list of buttons. Tapping one either opens another list of buttons, or does one specific thing. That is the whole structure.

Which means every job on this site is really the same job: find the right spot in the tree, add or change a button, tell it what to do, save, publish, test.

Keep in mind

Nobody in an emergency scrolls and hunts. Anything life-safety critical belongs on the home screen or one tap below it. Every level you add pushes something further away from someone who needs it.

How to add a button

The one you will use most. Everything else on this site is a variation of it.

  1. Decide where it goes before you open anything. Which existing list should it sit in? How many taps from the home screen? Anything life-safety belongs at home or one level down.
  2. In the Designer, click that list in the menu tree on the left. To put the button on the home screen, click the very top level.
  3. Click the control that adds a new item check the wording. A new blank button appears in the tree.
  4. Type the wording people will see on the button. Three words if you can manage it. See the Add a label section for how to word it well.
  5. Choose what the button does from the dropdown. Five choices, and each one has its own section on this site:
    • opens a list of more buttons
    • opens a page of information
    • dials a phone number
    • sends an email
    • opens a web page
  6. Fill in the matching box. The number, the email address, the web address, or the page content.
  7. Pick an icon if the Properties panel offers one, and make sure the button is set to visible.
  8. Click Save Properties.
  9. Look at the app preview on the right. Is the wording cut off? Is the button where you expected? Drag it into position if not.
  10. Publish it, then test it on a real phone. Both of those are in the Publish and test section.
Tip

If you are adding several related buttons, add the list button first, save it, then add the buttons underneath it. Building top down keeps the tree tidy and stops things landing at the wrong level.

How to add a label

The wording on a button, and the headings inside a page. Small thing, big difference.

The wording on a button

  1. In the menu tree, click the button you want to name or rename.
  2. In the Properties panel, find the box that holds the button's wording check the wording.
  3. Type the new label.
  4. Click Save Properties.
  5. Check the app preview. The label must fit on one line without being cut off. Older and smaller phones cut off sooner than the preview does, so if it only just fits, shorten it.
  6. Publish and test.

A heading inside a page

  1. Click the button whose page you want to add a heading to.
  2. In the content editor, put your cursor where the heading goes and type it.
  3. Select the text you just typed, then choose a heading style from the editor's formatting menu.
  4. Do not fake a heading by making text bold and big. A real heading style is what lets a screen reader announce it as a heading, which matters for anyone using the app with assistive technology.
  5. Click Save Properties, then read the page in the preview at phone width.

How to word a label well

  • Start with the verb. Call Security Services, not Security Services Contact Information.
  • Three words where possible, five at the absolute most.
  • Say what happens when it is tapped. Nobody should be surprised by where a button takes them.
  • No internal names or acronyms. Use the words a student would use, not the words on your org chart.
  • Sentence case, not CAPITALS. Capitals read slower, and they shout at someone who is already stressed.

How to add a page

A page of information: a procedure, a checklist, a set of instructions.

  1. Add a button in the right list, following the Add a button steps.
  2. Choose the option that opens a page of content check the wording.
  3. Open the content editor in the Properties panel. It works like a simplified Word: bold, bullets, headings, images and video.
  4. Put the action first. Do not open with background, policy or who owns the procedure. Open with what to do right now. Background goes at the bottom, if at all.
  5. Write in short numbered steps. One instruction per step.
  6. If you are copying from Word, paste as plain text and then apply formatting inside the editor. Pasting straight from Word drags in hidden styling that breaks the layout on phones.
  7. Add a short Reviewed: Month Year line at the bottom, so anyone reading can see it is current.
  8. Click Save Properties.
  9. Read the whole page in the app preview at phone width. A paragraph that looks fine on a laptop becomes a wall of text on a phone.
  10. Publish and test.
Why this matters

Content pages are the part of the app that works with no signal. If information has to be reachable when the network is down, it lives on a page like this, never behind a web link.

Never

Never keep two copies of the same procedure in different spots. They fall out of step, and one day someone reads the wrong one. One page, one source of truth. If you need it reachable from two places, use the Link inside the app section instead.

How to add a phone number

One tap and the phone starts dialling.

  1. Add a button in the right list, usually Emergency Contacts.
  2. Label it with the action and who answers. Call Security Services, not Security. The word "Call" tells someone exactly what the tap will do.
  3. Choose the option that places a call check the wording.
  4. Type the number with no spaces, dashes or brackets, and put the country code on the front:
    • Right: +17805550100
    • Wrong: 780-555-0100
    • Wrong: (780) 555 0100
    Punctuation and a missing country code are the usual reason a call button does nothing at all.
  5. If the number needs an extension, try a comma pause: +17805550100,,412. If the box will not accept it, put the extension in the button label instead so people can dial it themselves.
  6. Click Save Properties.
  7. Publish, then actually place the call from a real phone and confirm the right desk picks up. Do not skip this. A phone button is the single most important kind in the app.
Every three months

Dial every number in the app, by hand, once a quarter. Numbers change and nobody tells you. A dead emergency number is worse than no number, because the person stops looking for another way to get help.

How to add an email

One tap opens a new email, already addressed.

  1. Add a button in the right list.
  2. Label it with the action: Email the Wellness Team.
  3. Choose the option that sends an email check the wording.
  4. Use a monitored shared inbox, never one person's address. People change roles and leave. The button stays.
  5. If there is a subject line box, fill it in with something that helps the receiving team sort it, like App enquiry: Wellness. If there is a message body box, leave it empty or add a short prompt about what to include.
  6. Click Save Properties.
  7. Publish, then send a real test email from a phone and confirm it lands in the shared inbox. Warn the receiving team first so they do not treat it as a real request.
Never

Do not use an email button for anything urgent, or for anything that could be a disclosure. Nobody is watching an inbox at 2am. Urgent goes to a phone button, or to the app's built-in reporting feature, which routes and escalates properly.

How to add a web link

Send someone to a page on the university website.

  1. Open the page in a browser first and copy the address out of the address bar. Never type it from memory. Typos here are the most common broken button in any app.
  2. Add a button in the right list and label it for where it goes.
  3. Choose the option that opens a link check the wording.
  4. Paste the full address, including the https:// at the front.
  5. If you are offered a choice between opening inside the app or in the phone's browser: inside the app for short reference pages, in the browser for anything with a login or a form to fill in.
  6. Click Save Properties.
  7. Publish, then tap it on a real phone. The page must load and be readable at phone width. A desktop-only page is not worth linking to.
Never

Never put life-safety information behind a web link. Web links need a working signal, and a signal is exactly what you lose in the situations this app exists for. Critical procedures go on content pages.

Every three months

Tap every web link in the app once a quarter. Links break whenever the website team reorganises, and they will not think to tell you.

How to link to another spot in the app

Shortcuts and groupings, without making a second copy of anything.

The normal way: a button that opens a list

  1. Add a button where you want the doorway to be.
  2. Choose the option that opens a submenu or folder check the wording.
  3. Click Save Properties. You now have an empty list.
  4. With that button still selected in the tree, add the buttons that belong inside it. They appear underneath it, indented.
  5. Save, publish, test by tapping through from the home screen.

A shortcut to something that already exists

  1. Add a button where you want the shortcut.
  2. Look in the Properties panel for an option that points at an existing item or internal page check the wording.
  3. If it is there, use it and pick the destination. One page, two doorways, and you only ever have to update it once.
  4. If there is no such option, do not solve it by copying the page. Instead, move the page to whichever spot is more useful, and in the other spot put a short content page that says where to find it.
  5. Save, publish, then test by tapping through from both directions.
Never

Never duplicate life-safety content to save someone a tap. Two copies of the same emergency procedure will drift apart. This is the quietest way a safety app becomes wrong.

Rule of thumb

Two levels below the home screen is the limit. If you find yourself building a third, the structure is wrong. Move things up rather than burying them deeper.

How to add a picture

Maps, floor plans, diagrams and video.

  1. Resize the file before you upload it. Around 1000 pixels wide is plenty. A full size photo straight off a camera or phone makes the app slow to open, and slow is a failure in an emergency.
  2. Click the button whose page you are adding to, and open the content editor.
  3. Use the image or media control to upload the file.
  4. Add alternative text describing what the image shows, so the app works for someone using a screen reader.
  5. Click Save Properties.
  6. Check it in the app preview, then on a real phone. Floor plans and maps especially need a pinch to zoom test: if it cannot be zoomed and read, it is decoration, not information.
  7. Publish and test.
Never

Never put safety-critical instructions in a video only. Video streams from the internet, so it will not play with no signal. Write the steps out as text as well, every time.

How to move, hide or delete a button

Tidying up, seasonal content, and getting rid of things properly.

Move a button

  1. In the menu tree, drag the button up or down into position, or use the ordering control in Properties check how yours works.
  2. Order by urgency first, then by how often it is used. Emergency actions at the top, information at the bottom.
  3. Do not order alphabetically. Nobody in trouble reads alphabetically.
  4. Click Save Properties, check the preview, publish and test.

Hide a button

  1. Click the button, then switch its visibility off check the wording.
  2. It disappears from the app but stays in your dashboard, so you can bring it back in one click.
  3. This is the right answer for anything seasonal: orientation week, exam period, winter weather guidance, a construction detour.
  4. Click Save Properties, publish, and confirm on a phone that it is gone.

Delete a button

  1. Only delete if the content is wrong or obsolete and is never coming back. Otherwise hide it.
  2. Copy the page text somewhere safe first. Deleting is not reversible, and getting content back means a support ticket.
  3. Delete it, click Save Properties, publish.
  4. Check the whole surrounding list on a phone afterwards, in case anything else shifted.

How to publish and test

The last step of every job on this site. It takes about three minutes.

Publish

  1. Check that everything you changed has been saved with Save Properties.
  2. Walk the whole branch in the app preview one last time, the way a user would rather than the way you built it.
  3. Publish the change check the wording and where the control sits. It reaches phones in real time. There is no App Store submission, no review period, no waiting on IT.
  4. Test it straight away, using the checklist below.

Test on a real phone

  • Force close the app and reopen it, so it pulls the fresh content.
  • Find your change by navigating from the home screen, the way a user would, not by memory of where you put it.
  • The wording fits on one line and is not cut off.
  • Tap it. It does the thing it says it does.
  • Phone buttons: place the call and confirm the right desk answers.
  • Email buttons: send a real test and confirm it lands in the shared inbox.
  • Web buttons: the page loads and is readable at phone width.
  • Pages: read the whole thing on the phone. No wall of text, no broken images.
  • Check on both an iPhone and an Android phone. They lay things out differently.
  • Turn on airplane mode and confirm your emergency procedure pages still open.
  • Nothing else moved or vanished while you were in there.
Worth doing

Keep one iPhone and one Android in the office with the app installed. Testing should never be blocked by not having the right handset in the room.

How to undo something

  1. Change the field back to what it was and publish again. Publishing a correction is faster and safer than hunting for a restore button.
  2. For something you cannot fix by editing, such as a deleted page you need back, raise a ticket on the dashboard's Support page.
